How it works

  1. Someone reports it. Type /shift in the group chat your team already uses, or send a report link. No Buffer login for them.
  2. You see the clash. The report sits beside today's and this week's posts. Anything that mentions the same thing gets flagged.
  3. You act in one place. Move a post to draft, post an update to the channels you pick, or leave a note for whoever's on next.

What it does for you

Catch it before it publishes

A report that matches a scheduled post shows up next to that post, labeled "might clash," so you decide what happens to it.

Buffer stays in charge

Buffer is still where your posts live. Shift never publishes on its own, and every public action waits for you to confirm it.

Put it back when it's fixed

Move a post to draft and restore it later at its original time. Nobody has to rebuild the schedule by hand.

Handoffs that don't need a phone tree

Leave notes for the next shift, keep a log of what happened, and copy a summary built only from recorded actions.

Built for two kinds of teams

Zoos, museums and attractions

Your staff know first. Your posts should catch up.

A ride goes down, a gate closes early, a keeper talk moves, a lost child is found. Front-line staff report it from the group they already use, and Shift puts it beside the post that's about to go out.

  • Mark urgent reports with ! and they go to the top
  • Staff without GroupMe can use a report link
  • Notes carry across shifts, so the next person knows what's still open

SaaS teams with one social person

Engineering knows about the outage. The feed should too.

When there's an incident or a bug everyone is talking about, the people who know post it in chat. Shift shows it next to the scheduled product posts, so the social lead can hold, reword or send a status update without checking five tools.

  • Outages and bugs reported straight from chat
  • Scheduled product posts checked against what's broken right now
  • A handoff summary for whoever covers the next time zone
